Move library
Each move is a ready-made set of key poses: anticipation, contact, follow-through, recovery. Click one to replace the current action's frames and its loop duration; Ctrl+click appends it to the end instead, so you can chain moves into a longer combo. Poses come from the built-in atlas, but any pose you save in the library under the same name overrides it. Off-hand swaps the leading side, heavy exaggerates every angle and slows the loop, quick tightens and speeds it up. Sword and dash moves carry their own per-segment timing (in-between count, easing and hold frames per key), following the chamber → overshoot → settle → recovery rules from the animation tutorials; the rest switch in-betweens on for every frame. Save action as move puts the action you are editing into this library — every key pose, its in-betweens, easing, holds and the loop duration — under a name you choose, and asks whether to generate its off-hand, heavy and quick variants. Saving under a built-in name replaces that move in the list; deleting your copy puts the built-in back. The ✕ on a plain built-in row deletes it permanently — no undo, no restore. Saved moves live in this browser. Export offers a .json to save, and Import reads one back — pick a file, or paste a moves file into the Export tab's JSON box and press Import.

Reference track
Drag a joint on the stage to aim that bone. Every handle aims its own bone on a plain drag; press Tab to switch on skeleton mode, where dragging a hand or foot solves that limb as a linked chain. Upper bones use world angles; forearm and shin angles are relative to their parent. Shoulder pivots (green) and hip pivots (amber) drag the same way; the Rig tab sets their base position. Hold Ctrl while dragging a joint to stretch that bone instead of rotating it. Hold Alt and the handle snaps onto the same joint's position in the previous or next keyframe — the blue and orange rings show those targets. The orange handle at the weapon tip swings the weapon about the grip, rolls it on its own axis with Ctrl held, or with Alt held stretches and aims it together so the tip tracks the cursor; the violet handle at the chest drives sp (lower spine), while the neck handle drives sp2. The orange pelvis pivot at the hips moves the figure, or with Ctrl held rotates the upper body about the pelvis; the amber root handle above the head moves the whole figure, or with Ctrl held spins the entire character about its hips through a single rot value, leaving every other pose number untouched.
Every layer is one bone between two named pivots — the same names the pose-match landmarks use (shL → elL is the far upper arm, knR → ftR the near shin), so marking a joint and re-stacking the limb it belongs to refer to the same thing. Draw order and visibility are stored per keyframe, so a limb can cross in front of the torso on one frame and behind it on the next. Top of the list draws last (nearest the viewer). Drag a row by its grip (or anywhere but the checkbox and arrows) to restack it, or use ▲/▼ — both change this keyframe only; “Copy to all frames” pushes the order everywhere.
Layer mask (transport bar) tints each part by depth: #F2F2F2 at the top of the stack down to #BEA0FF at the bottom.
Layers are a Rig Forge authoring construct. The loader rasterises each keyframe into the FrameBundle as one figure, so order and visibility bake into the rendered frame rather than exporting as engine data.
Auto in-between generates motion from a keyframe into the next one. Angles take the shortest way round, so a joint sitting at 175° in one frame and -175° in the next travels 10° rather than spinning 350° the other way, and two identical values hold perfectly still. Pick a different rotation path when you actually want the long way, a full turn, or the old raw numeric behaviour. It is a per-frame toggle — turn it on where a move should flow and leave it off where a snap reads better.
apply to a range
The loader plays keyframes as authored with no interpolation. In-betweens therefore have to be baked into the keyframe list on export — the Export tab does that when "bake in-betweens" is ticked. Leaving it unticked exports only your authored keyframes.
angle winding — per pivot
Every angle has two numbers for the same position: 175° and −185° put a limb in exactly the same place. Tick a pivot to swap it to its other number — on the keyframe you are viewing by default, or across the whole action with the box below ticked. The figure does not move, but the in-between now travels the other way round, and that pivot's value is then followed literally instead of being shortest-arced.

pose match
Pose match reads the image as a silhouette: it thresholds light against dark, keeps the largest connected shape and treats its farthest boundary points as head, hands and feet. That is reliable for stick figures, sprites and clean cut-outs, and unreliable for photographs, busy backgrounds or overlapping limbs. Mark points accepts fifteen landmarks — head, chest, both shoulders, elbows, hands, hip centre, both hips, knees and feet — and any you skip are taken from the same joint in the previous and next keyframes when that guide is on, or otherwise solved from the ones you placed. While you mark, the joint you are placing shows its position in the previous frame (blue ring), the next frame (orange ring) and the midpoint between them; clicking near any of the three snaps onto it, so a joint that should not move between frames stays put exactly. It is a starting pose to nudge, not a measurement — and it never reads depth, so which hand is near or far is a guess from left/right position.
Reference images are an authoring aid held in this browser only. They are not part of the pb_character schema and never appear in an export.

Handles behave normally on a plain drag — each aims its own bone. Press Tab (or click the Skeleton button in the transport bar) to switch the mode on. While it is on, dragging a hand or foot solves the whole limb instead of aiming one bone, and dragging the pelvis carries the torso while the legs bend to keep both feet planted where they were: the elbow bends behind the arm and the knee ahead of the leg — each of the four joints has its own invert checkbox when a pose wants the other solution. Reach past the limb's length and assist decides how much the rest of the body comes with it — the pelvis carries most of the load and the spine leans the remainder, so the figure answers like a weighted branched linkage instead of a stretched stick.
Changing scale or pose extents also moves hurtbox_radius, which the loader derives from the idle-pose joint extent.

PNG frames
Each frame renders onto a transparent square: the rig as a solid black silhouette, the weapon drawn exactly as on the stage, with each keyframe's own layer order and hidden layers. No grid, handles, onion skin or reference images. The bake in-betweens box above decides the frames — ticked exports the full playback sequence (keys, in-betweens and holds), unticked only your authored keyframes. One scale is fitted to the largest extent across all of this character's actions, so the figure is the same size in every PNG whichever button you press; each action is centred on its own motion, and every frame of an action shares that origin, so the frames stack as a sprite sequence. face left mirrors the export too. Files are named <action>_000.png upward in playback order.
character package (game + FX Studio)
Writes one folder per character: every action's frames as <action>_NN.png on one shared square canvas sized to fit every move at the chosen head size (in-betweens included, played at the action's ms/frame) plus character.json with the name, archetype, stats, palette, per-action timing and starting anchor points (hands, blade tip, head, feet…). Pick the game's characters folder: a <name> sub-folder is created (or pick that sub-folder itself). Old <action>_NN.png files of the exported actions are replaced. Open the same folder in the FX Studio to build the character's FX. Opened from disk in Chrome or Edge it writes straight into the folder you pick; anywhere else (this published page, Firefox) it saves one <name>.zip holding the <name>/ folder — unzip it into characters/. Frames use the same solid silhouette, layer order and hidden layers as the PNG export above.
